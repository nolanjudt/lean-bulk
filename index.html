How to Read ItInterpretation
The formula: TDEE = average intake − (weight change × 3,500 ÷ days)

• Weeks 1–2 are throwaway data. Glycogen and water are refilling. Expect 2–4 lbs up. This is not fat and does not mean the plan failed.
• Read the 7-day rolling average, never a single day. Daily readings are dominated by glycogen, water and gut contents.
• Do NOT judge progress by the body-fat % reading. It read 15.7% on Sep 7 and 18.1% on Sep 15 — a 2.4-point swing in eight days. Bioimpedance tracks hydration, not fat. Target is 142.0 lb rolling average by Oct 16, with logged lifts holding steady.
• Losing 0.6–0.7 lb/week with strength holding → lean phase working as intended. This is the target state.
• Losing more than ~1 lb/week → deficit too aggressive to hold lean mass. Add 150–250 cal.
• Weight flat → not actually in a deficit. Cut 150–250 cal.
• Strength dropping across sessions → losing lean mass, not just fat. Raise calories — protein is already at 175g.

Strength on your anchor lifts is the better signal than the scale. If Leg Press and Machine Bench Press are climbing while weight holds, the composition is moving even when the number doesn't.

Macro Ordering RulesThese Govern
The meal cards above are templates, not prescriptions. When they conflict with these rules, the rules win.

Priority order (Sun–Thu):
1. Calories are a hard ceiling. Nothing overrides this.
2. Protein floor (~160g) is the priority inside it. Overage is fine only if it fits under the ceiling — it never pushes calories over.
3. Fat floor next, for hormonal health. Fat outranks carbs.
4. Carbs are the lever — they absorb whatever room is left.

Friday: carbs get priority, but fat holds a hard 45g minimum.
Saturday: carbs take full precedence; fat may run under floor.

Both exceptions exist to fuel the Sat legs / Sun full upper anchors. If the anchors move, revisit the rule.
⚖️ The rest-day target can't be hit exactly. 155g protein + 99g carbs + 54g fat sums to 1,502 calories against a 1,490 ceiling — it's over-specified. Following the priority order lands you at 1,490 cal · 155g P · 54g F · ~89g C. Carbs absorb the shortfall, which is exactly what the rule says should happen.
🍠 Why the dinner potato is the lever: carbs are the residual, so they belong in one adjustable place rather than scattered across fixed items. Scale the potato to hit the ceiling after protein and fat are covered.
🥚 Why breakfast is split. Four whole eggs carried 21g of fat, which crowded carbs out against the rest-day calorie ceiling. Two eggs plus 200g of yogurt holds the same protein for half the fat, and the freed room went into the dinner potato.
⏰ Early-AM sessions are fueled by the night before. A 5:30 AM lift runs on Thursday's dinner, not Friday's banana. Load carbs at the previous night's dinner — that's exactly why Friday is a carb-priority day feeding Saturday's legs.
Levers & MeasurementStanding Rules
Almonds are the calorie lever, always weighed — 15g Phase 1 training · 35g Phase 2 training. (Rest days get their fat from dinner oil instead.)
CorePower and Quest bars are backups, not defaults — cost, and whole food is the preference. Max one CorePower on the days you do use one.
Weigh meat and rice cooked, never raw or dry. Jasmine rice expands ~2.8× dry to cooked.

Tracking:
• Weigh in daily on waking, same conditions. Compare weekly averages only, never day to day.
• Use active calories from the watch, not total — total includes resting burn already inside maintenance.
• Wrist HR underestimates resistance training. Treat lifting burn as a floor, not a measurement.
• Sick days, travel weeks, and procedure prep are excluded from averages entirely.
📅 Friday mornings: you know by Thursday bedtime whether the gym happens. If it's on, carb-load Thursday dinner — the 5:30 AM session runs on that, not on Friday's breakfast. Friday still defaults to a rest day otherwise.
What Changed From the CutOrientation
• Rest days stopped being punishment days. They ran 17g of carbs on the cut. Now dinner carries a real potato portion — you're recovering on those days, not just surviving them.
• Almonds are the new lever. Adding 300+ calories through more chicken or rice means more volume and more prep. 20–40g of almonds does it in a handful and travels in your bag.
• Protein didn't move. 155–164g across every day type, both phases. It's the one thing you shouldn't flex.
• Lunch and breakfast are untouched. The larb, the Greek bowl, the eggs, the two-batch prep cadence — all identical. You're not relearning your week.

The Weekly RhythmFollow This

Larb batch + beef + eggs
Larb splits into 3 portions covering Mon, Tue, Wed lunches. Brown the second beef pack and hard-boil a dozen-plus eggs while the larb cools — those eggs are every non-training breakfast.
Tuesday — the short cook (~35 min)
Greek chicken batch
Roast, portion, done. Splits into 3 covering Thu, Fri lunches with one spare for Saturday or a missed meal.
✅ Why this split: larb is best inside 3–4 days, so a Sunday batch stretched to Friday strands the last portion. Cooking chicken Tuesday means every lunch you eat is within four days of cooking — and Wednesday's the only day you'd ever have both in the fridge.
Sunday Prep (~60 min)Covers Mon–Wed
1. Thai Beef Larb — full batch (~25 min)
  • Toast 15g coconut flakes in a dry sprayed skillet, 2–3 min, stirring constantly — pull the moment it goes golden
  • Brown one full pack (605g) of 93% lean over medium, breaking it fine, 5–6 min — the recipe is scaled to use the whole pack, no leftovers to deal with
  • Off heat, add 45g fish sauce, 38g lime juice, 5g monkfruit, 4–6g chili flakes
  • Cool 2–3 min, then fold in shallots, mint, cilantro, green onions, toasted coconut
  • Divide into 3 containers (250g each) → three weekday lunches
2. Brown Dinner Beef — the second pack
  • Second full pack (605g) in a skillet, medium-high, 8–10 min. Still drain it — leaner, but not dry
  • 605g raw cooks down to roughly 480g, which portions into 3 × 140g dinner blocks with a little left over
  • Those 3 blocks cover Sat, Sun, and one weekday training dinner
  • Scrape the extra ~60g into a container — it's a free topper for a salad or the spare Greek bowl
3. Hard-Boil Eggs — this is breakfast now
  • Boil 12–16 eggs: cover with water, bring to a boil, kill the heat, cover 11 min
  • Straight into an ice bath for 5 min — this is what makes them peel cleanly
  • Leave shells on until you eat them. Peeled eggs go rubbery in 2 days; unpeeled keep a week
  • 4 per breakfast on every non-training day, plus spares for a skipped meal
✅ Three portions, three days — Mon/Tue/Wed. Nothing gets pushed past day four now that Tuesday's chicken covers the back half of the week.
🥩 Beef math: your packs are 1.333 lb (605g). Pack 1 goes entirely into the larb — the recipe was built around that exact weight. Pack 2 browns down to ~480g, which is 3 dinner portions. Two packs is the standard week; buy a third only if you hit the gym twice midweek and both weekend days.
✅ Lettuce cups stay separate. Pack the larb in the container and the leaves in a bag — assembling early makes them weep and go limp by noon.
Tuesday Prep — Greek Chicken (~35 min)Covers Thu–Fri
1. Marinate & Roast the Chicken (460g raw)
  • Toss 460g raw chicken breast with 15g olive oil, juice of 1 lemon, 3 cloves minced garlic, 2 tsp dried oregano, salt and pepper
  • Marinate 20 min while you build everything else — longer is fine but not required
  • Roast 400°F for 18–22 min until 165°F internal. Rest 10 min before slicing or it dries out in the container
  • Slice and portion into 3 containers (~115g cooked each)
2. Tzatziki (300g nonfat Greek yogurt)
  • Grate 100g cucumber, then squeeze it dry in a towel — skip this and the sauce turns to water by Wednesday
  • Stir into 300g nonfat Greek yogurt with 1 clove garlic, chopped dill, lemon juice, salt
  • Portion 100g into separate small containers, not into the bowl
3. Salad Component
  • Chop 100g cucumber and 50g red onion
  • Add 30g kalamata olives and 45g crumbled feta, split across the 3 containers
  • No dressing — the tzatziki is the dressing when you assemble at lunch
⚠️ Pack tzatziki separately every time. Chicken and salad hold 4–5 days; yogurt sauce sitting on cucumber does not.
✅ Third portion is your buffer — use it Saturday, or as backup if a weekday meal gets skipped. Chicken and salad hold 4–5 days, so it keeps comfortably.
